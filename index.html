Quantum Mechanics: Wave-Particle Duality

1. The Classical Paradox (Wave Behavior)

When electrons or photons pass through two slits without being observed, each quantum entity exhibits wave properties. Its probability wavefunction $\psi$ passes through both slits simultaneously, causing constructive and destructive interference:

$$I(y) \propto |\psi_1 + \psi_2|^2 = |\psi_1|^2 + |\psi_2|^2 + 2\text{Re}(\psi_1^*\psi_2)$$

This creates alternating bright and dark interference fringes on the phosphor detection screen.

2. The Quantum Measurement Problem (Detector ON)

When we place a "which-way" detector at the slits to observe which slit the particle went through, the quantum superposition collapses into an eigenstate (wavefunction collapse):

$$I_{\text{observed}}(y) = |\psi_1|^2 + |\psi_2|^2$$

The interference cross-term $2\text{Re}(\psi_1^*\psi_2)$ completely vanishes! The particles behave strictly as classical bullets, forming two separate localized clumps on the screen directly behind each slit.
